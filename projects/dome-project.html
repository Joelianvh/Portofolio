Dome Project

A 360° spatial game developed in 5 weeks for the Planetarium at Artis Amsterdam, combining real-time gameplay with custom hardware controls.

My Role Lead programmer (5 weeks)
Tech & tools
Unity (C#) Artis Planetarium VR prototyping Modified flight stick 360° spatial projection
Links Watch video showcase

Context & problem

Developed for the Planetarium at Artis Amsterdam in 5 weeks. Designing for a dome meant rethinking spatial UI, player orientation and how gameplay works on a 360° canvas without screen edges.

Target audience

Planetarium visitors, museum audiences and creative technology researchers interested in non-traditional displays.

Features

360° projection-ready camera rendering in Unity, VR setup for spatial previews, physical interaction through a modified flight stick, and integrated audio.

Process

As lead programmer, I built the dome rendering pipeline in Unity, wired the modified flight stick, and used VR headsets during development to test the 360° player perspective before moving to the real dome projector.

Takeaways

Designing for non-standard displays taught me to adapt camera systems to spatial environments, and that physical controls like a flight stick make 360° navigation feel natural.
